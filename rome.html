1. Saint Peter’s Square

Bernini’s grand design Gian Lorenzo Bernini shaped the square for Pope Alexander VII, with two great colonnades reaching out like open arms to welcome everyone in. Built to be walked through * A trapezoidal front section frames the basilica façade. * A huge elliptical space stretches about 240 meters * As you move, the columns seem to shift and open the view. The obelisk at the center * Red granite, quarried in Aswan in ancient Egypt. * Brought to Rome by Emperor Caligula in 37 CE. * Pope Sixtus V moved it here in 1586, before the square existed. The two fountains * The northern one was designed by Carlo Maderno in 1614. * Bernini added a matching southern fountain in 1677 for symmetry. The colonnade illusion to find Look for the round pavement marker reading Centro del Colonnato. Stand right on it, and Bernini’s four rows of columns line up into what looks like a single ring. Do try it. Best photo spots * On a marker disc, the four rows collapse into one line. * Step back to line up the obelisk, fountains, and dome.
